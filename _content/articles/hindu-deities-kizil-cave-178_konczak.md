> The eclectic iconographic borrowings in the depiction of deities in Kizil show that the artists of Kucha developed a new, autonomous iconography based on the tradition of neighbouring regions.

An iconographic and textual analysis of a silk road wall painting from Kizil Cave 178 depicting Hindu deities (such as Śiva, Viṣṇu, Brahmā, and Indra) surrounding the Buddha. Konczak traces artistic influences from Gandhara, Mathura, Gupta India, Sogdiana, and Kashmir, proposing that the depiction functions as a visual allusion to protective texts like the *Mahāsamājasūtra*.

This image, of the Buddha surrounded by protecting dieties, strikingly shows how the parittas of Indian Buddhism evolved into the elaborate maṇḍalas of the later Mahāyāna.
