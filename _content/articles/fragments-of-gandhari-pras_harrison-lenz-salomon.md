> … buddha-fields completely filled with all 
treasures; and whatever gentleman or lady …
would trust, believe, accept, and be devoted to [it]; and having 
trusted, believed …
might teach to others in full, reveal, and cultivate [it], thereby, householder, this gentleman or lady …

Some pictures, transcriptions, and translations of a few fragments of a Mahāyāna Sūtra found in Gāndhāra which prove that Mahāyāna Buddhism existed in Northwest India in the first centuries of the common era alongside the Śrāvakayāna.
Their differences from the extant Chinese and Tibetan translations of this sutra also show that these Mahāyāna texts were still somewhat plastic at this early date and suggest that the Chinese Sūtras were (sometimes?) translated not directly from Sanskrit but from an intermediate, Central Asian language.
