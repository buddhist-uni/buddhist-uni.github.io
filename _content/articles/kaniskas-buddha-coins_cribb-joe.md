> Kaniṣka's faith involved devotion to Śākyamuni and Maitreya, who were looked upon by him as having quasi-divine status, by means of devotional images.

In this article, Joe Cribb examines the Buddhist imagery found on the reverse side of coins issued by the Kushan King Kaniṣka.
By closely analyzing these numismatic artifacts alongside contemporary Mathuran and Gandharan sculpture, Cribb demonstrates that the coins provide concrete evidence for the early development of Buddhist iconography.
In particular, the identification of a seated figure as Maitreya Buddha (rather than a Bodhisattva) shows how the Mahāyāna cultic practices evolved from earlier devotions.
