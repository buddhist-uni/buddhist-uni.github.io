> The precision of Ellora's compositions strongly suggests that worship in 
the shrines could have been viewed as a substitution or transposition of 
worship at Bodhgaya

> Ellora appears to reflect esoteric Buddhism on the cusp of change, at a relatively early point.

Malandra reads the layout and sculptural programs of Ellora’s 8th-century Buddhist caves as visual evidence of the early development of the Maṇḍala.

Taking these late, Southwestern caves seriously as a pilgrimage site in their own right, Malandra sees them not as the "dead-end" of Buddhism in India but as the pivot point between early Indian Buddhism centered on Śākyamuni Buddha, and medieval, esoteric Buddhism centered everywhere.
Thus, in seeing the Maṇḍala in Ellora, we can come to see Ellora's place in the greater Maṇḍala of Buddhist history.
