> With a correct genealogy established, it will be possible to observe how the text changed and expanded through time, and how its purpose, message, and method of teaching evolved.

Applying the stemmatic methods of textual criticism, Morten Schlütter maps the family tree (*stemma codicum*) of the *Platform Sūtra* (*Liùzǔ tánjīng* 六祖壇經) attributed to the Sixth Patriarch Huìnéng (惠能).

Comparing the early Dūnhuáng (敦煌) manuscript, the two-fascicle Huìxīn (惠昕) lineage preserved in Japan, and the longer Song–Yuan canonical versions of Déyì (德異) and Zōngbǎo (宗寶), he shows how textual contamination complicates the earlier recensions. He argues that the longer versions derived not from Qìsōng's (契嵩) lost edition, but from a Kōshōji-type text expanded with encounter dialogues drawn from the *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù* (景德傳燈錄) and *Zōngmén liándēng huìyào* (宗門聯燈會要).

Note that this article focuses strictly on textual genealogy, bibliographical history, and variant readings—including extensive Chinese character appendices—rather than the doctrinal contents of the *Platform Sūtra*.
