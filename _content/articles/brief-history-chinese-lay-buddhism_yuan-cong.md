> Laypeople’s responsibility for Buddhism is far beyond financial and organizational support. They must negotiate with power, debate with criticizers, and interpret the doctrine in a most acceptable way for local people, together with the Saṅgha.

Yuan traces how lay Buddhists helped Buddhism take root in China from its early introduction through the Song dynasty, focusing on their mediation between the monastic saṅgha, imperial authorities, Confucian and Daoist critics, and local society.
