> The transformation of the two truths theory from a simple hermeneutic strategy to a complex system of thought with highly sophisticated ontological, epistemological and semantic theories blurring [sic] a clear methodological distinction between 'reality' and 'truth'.

The article illustrates that the development of Mahāyāna Buddhism was driven by intense dialectical debates over the ontological status of reality, specifically framed through the evolving doctrine of the two truths: conventional and ultimate.
It demonstrates that Mahāyāna thought developed in direct opposition to the foundationalist substance-metaphysics of the Abhidharma schools, such as the Vaibhāṣika and Sautrāntika, pushing Buddhist philosophy toward increasingly sophisticated forms of idealism and anti-foundationalism.
