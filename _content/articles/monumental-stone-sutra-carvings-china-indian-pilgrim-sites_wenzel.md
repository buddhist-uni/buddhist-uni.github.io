> But was it really the novel which inspired the literati to create the name of “Rock for Sunning the Sūtra”?
Or had this name already been transmitted for a long time, and, vice versa, it was the site of Mount Tai that inspired the author of the *Journey to the West*?

In this article, Wenzel explores how medieval Chinese Buddhists may have modeled their pilgrimage sites on ancient, South Asian antecedents.
Specifically, she suggests that the Diamond Sūtra's carving at Mount Tai in the 6th century mirrored the sites where the Buddha washed and sunned his robe which were popular pilgrimage sites in 5th century India.

The article offers an excellent overview of ancient Buddhist relic worship and the creation of sacred geography, but its core argument (connecting the two sites) is based on questionable evidence,
most prominently the Ming-dynasty "Commentary" carved over the top of the Sūtra by Confucian aristocrats a millennium later.
How plausible do you find it, her hypothesis that these characters (accidentally?) preserve the site's ancient, Buddhist meaning?
