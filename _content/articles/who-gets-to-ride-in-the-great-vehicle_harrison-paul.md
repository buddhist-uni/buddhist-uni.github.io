> The first thing that strikes one when reading these early Mahāyāna sūtras is their extreme defensiveness. The texts fairly groan under the weight of their own self-glorification...

Analyzing the eleven earliest Chinese translations of Mahāyāna sūtras, Harrison dismisses the ideas that the early Mahāyāna was a lay movement, a movement for gender equality, or a "cult of celestial Bodhisattvas."
It was rather a minority view among the Buddhist schools that saw the aspiration to Buddhahood as the highest goal and who sought to justify that attitude "with a truly prodigious degree of polemical overkill."
