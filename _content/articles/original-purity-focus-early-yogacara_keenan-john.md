> If the mind is originally pure, then how is one to account for empirical defilement? If the mind is not itself pure, then, being defiled, how can one ever attain purification?

In this article, John P. Keenan compares the doctrinal emphases of four early, pre-classical Yogācāra texts: the *Mahāyāna Sūtrā Laṃkāra*, *Saṃdhinirmocanasūtra*, *Mahāyānābhidharmasūtra*, and *Madhyānta Vibhāga Śāstra*.
He argues that early Yogācāra developed through attempts to reconcile the Tathāgatagarbha teaching of original mental purity with the more empirical (abhidharmic?) analysis of defilements and consciousness.
