> The Toyok paintings cannot be explained by merely referring to the *Visualization Sūtra* alone but can be interpreted only by looking at the entire body of relevant meditation manuals.

In this article, Nobuyoshi Yamabe examines the mural paintings of visualizing monks and related imagery in Caves 20 and 42 at Toyok, the oldest Buddhist cave-temple site in the Turfan area. By comparing the paintings and inscriptions with the Visualization Sūtra (觀無量壽經 [Taishō, vol. 12, no. 365]),, the Ocean Sūtra (觀佛三昧海經 [Taishō, vol. 15, no. 643]), the Secret Essentials (禪秘要法經 [Taishō, vol. 15, no. 613]), the Cures (治禪病秘要法 [Taishō, vol. 15, no. 630]), the Five Gates (五門禪經要用法 [Taishō, vol. 15, no. 610]), and the Sanskrit *Yogalehrbuch*, he argues that the murals preserve evidence for a Central Asian practice context behind these Chinese sūtras and the development of the Pure Land visualizations.
