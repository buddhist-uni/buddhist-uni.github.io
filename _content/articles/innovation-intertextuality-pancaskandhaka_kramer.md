> By providing examples for various forms of borrowings and additions this study aims at exploring the amount of innovation and the role of intertextuality in [supposedly commentarial] texts, thereby contributing to our understanding of the functions of Indian commentaries as well as of the motives and compositional techniques of their authors.

In this article, Jowita Kramer examines Vasubandhu's *Pañcaskandhaka* and its commentaries to show how Indian religious authors creatively integrated, modified, and recast earlier teachings to further their own agendas.
It provides an excellent example of how, while appearing conservative, the act of quoting and "explaining" earlier texts was often a ceative act.
