> As far as ordinary people (*pṛthagjana*) are concerned, the world which they see and reflect in their language is inaccurate and ultimately illusory. In this sense, one could say that in the case of ordinary man, the only thing which actually exists is his mind. Though the *Bodhisattvabhūmi* authors do not use such terms or descriptions, it seems that an incipient idea of 'mind-only' was, so to speak, almost on the tip of their tongues.

An examination of the contemplative practices and epistemological foundations of the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*, focusing on its concepts of the "thing-in-itself" (*vastumātra*), the four investigations (*paryeṣaṇā*), and the four correct cognitions (*yathābhūtaparijñāna*). Florin Deleanu demonstrates how this early text acts as a bridge between Prajñāpāramitā philosophy and later Vijñānavāda idealism.
